Boomerang

Your iPhone and your Apple account, at home on elementary OS.

Download .deb Source code

Free software for elementary OS 8+. No account, no server, no telemetry. Formerly Covalence.

Screenshots with made-up data. English is in beta.

iPhone connection

Over Bluetooth, directly between your computer and your iPhone.

Notifications

Every iPhone notification on your desktop, with its actions and the icon of the app that sent it. You pick the apps.

Messages

Read your SMS conversations, reply to one person, keep drafts, search. Delete from Boomerang without touching the iPhone.

Phone

Answer and place calls with the computer's microphone and speakers. Dial pad and call history.

Contacts

Your iPhone contacts over Bluetooth, or your iCloud contacts, which you can edit.

AirPods

Battery of each bud and the case, noise control, conversation awareness, ear detection.

Now Playing

Cover, progress and volume of what the iPhone plays, and a mini player above Settings. Play starts the iPhone's music again.

Sound and battery

The iPhone's audio on your speakers, its battery in the panel.

Apple Services connection

Over the Internet, between your computer and iCloud, in the apps you already use.

Mail, calendars, reminders, contacts

Added to elementary's apps through Evolution Data Server, with an app-specific password.

iCloud Drive

A folder in Files, powered by rclone.

iCloud Photos

Your albums in Files, read only, powered by rclone.

About iCloud Drive and Photos. rclone signs in the way icloud.com does, with your Apple Account password and two-factor authentication. Apple does not offer this access officially: the iCloud terms limit automated access and allow Apple to suspend an account. Advanced Data Protection must be turned off, which reduces the end-to-end encryption of your iCloud data. The sign-in token expires about once a month. Use it at your own risk.

Install

  1. Download boomerang_0.8.2-1_amd64.deb.
  2. Install it from a terminal: sudo apt install ./boomerang_0.8.2-1_amd64.deb. apt also installs the packages it needs. On Ubuntu, the App Center can also open it with a double-click.
  3. Log out and back in, then open Boomerang. The setup assistant walks you through pairing your iPhone and signing in to iCloud.

Something missing later? Boomerang lists it and installs it with one click. A built-in guide, in English and French, explains every step (press F1).

Questions

Can I send iMessages?

No. Bluetooth only lets a computer send an SMS to one person. Group replies and attachments stay on the iPhone.

What about the universal clipboard, Handoff or AirDrop?

Not possible: they need Apple's own encryption and Wi-Fi stack.

Where do my messages go?

Nowhere but your computer. Boomerang has no server and sends no statistics. Read the privacy notice.

My iPhone does not see the computer in its Bluetooth settings.

First click “Pair an iPhone…” in Boomerang's Overview: the computer stays visible for three minutes under Settings › Bluetooth › Other Devices. If it still does not show up, turn the iPhone's Bluetooth off and on again. The built-in guide covers every step.

Made by an enthusiast

I am not a developer. I am an elementary OS fan with a few ideas and an iPhone in my pocket, and I build Boomerang by "vibe coding" with Claude, Anthropic's AI assistant. I describe what I want, test it every day on my own computer, and we fix things together. The code is open so that people who know better can read it, point out mistakes and help. Issues and kind advice are very welcome.
